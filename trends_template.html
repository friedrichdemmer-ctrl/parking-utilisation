    How this was measured

    • Occupancy is 1 − free ÷ capacity, sampled every hour from each garage's latest reading (carried forward for up to 12 hours) and averaged per calendar month.
    • A garage-month is left out when samples cover less than half the month, more than 5% of readings fall outside 0–110% of capacity, the feed repeats the same few values, or the garage looks full around the clock (a stuck counter).
    • Seasonality and the weekday comparison use garages that reported in every month (or at least six months) of the year in question.
    • City figures compare each garage's month with the same month a year earlier, weighted by capacity, so a garage counts only for months it reported in both years.
    • Car-park types come from garage names: "Galerie", "Arcaden" or "Einkaufszentrum" mean a shopping centre, "Klinikum" a hospital, "Hauptbahnhof" a station, "Messe" or "Theater" an event venue. Garages named after a street or square count as city centre.

    Read with care

    • Almost all of the long history comes from German cities (plus Basel), via the community parking-data archive. Other countries join in 2026 and do not yet affect the trend.
    • The shopping-centre group has only about two dozen garages with long histories, so one large centre (the Regensburg Arcaden, 1,548 spaces) moves it noticeably.
    • A change in one large car park can move a small city's figure. Where a city has only a few garages, look at the garage count before reading much into it.
    • Event dates and descriptions come from the sources linked in the list; rules differed by state, so dates are the federal ones. The effect shown for an event is only the change in the index over the three months from it against the three before, seasonally adjusted. It is a statement about the chart, not about causes: several things change at once, and the index sees garages, not people's reasons.
